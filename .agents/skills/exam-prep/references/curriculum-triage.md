# Curriculum Triage & Time-Budgeting Guide

This reference defines the mathematical and heuristic rules for triaging a course syllabus based on remaining time until exam day, guaranteeing maximum mark yield under time pressure.

---

## 1. Time Horizon Classification

Calculate Remaining Days $D = \text{Exam Date} - \text{Today's Date}$.

| Category | Time Remaining | Primary Objective | Target Mark Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Emergency Crunch** | $1 \le D \le 7$ days | Safe Pass / 50–65% | Master Tier 1 (top 2–3 chapters) + Golden Formulas & Definitions for Tier 2. Zero non-essential reading. |
| **Tactical Sprint** | $8 \le D \le 30$ days | First Class / 65–80% | Deep mastery of Tier 1 + High-Leverage Foundation of Tier 2 + 2 full mock question drills. |
| **Comprehensive Mastery** | $D > 30$ days | Distinction / 85–100% | Full Tier 1 & Tier 2 coverage + Tier 3 edge cases + Spaced Repetition + Timed writing practice. |

---

## 2. The 3-Tier Pareto Curriculum Architecture

Never advise a student to simply "skip" chapters when time is short. Skipping whole chapters leaves blind spots on compulsory short questions (Section A). Instead, use the **Tiered Coverage System**:

### Tier 1: The Core High-Yield (60% to 70% of Marks)
- **Selection Criteria:** The 20% to 30% of chapters/modules that historically carry 60%+ of the total marks in past papers.
- **Depth:** 100% Depth.
- **Coverage:** Full conceptual understanding, derivations, edge-case problem variations, past 5 years' exact exam questions, step-by-step master answers.
- **Time Allocated:** 60% of total available study hours.

### Tier 2: The High-Leverage Safety Net (20% to 30% of Marks)
- **Selection Criteria:** Remaining chapters in the syllabus that appear as individual questions or in compulsory short-answer sections.
- **Depth:** 40% Depth (Foundational Axioms).
- **Coverage:**
  - The 3 most fundamental definitions.
  - The 2 core formulas / classification rules.
  - 1 standard plug-and-chug problem.
  - High-level intuitive grasp of what the chapter is about.
- **Outcome:** If a question appears on the exam from these chapters, the student will NOT get a blank zero; they will comfortably answer standard questions or pick up 70–80% partial marks on complex ones.
- **Time Allocated:** 30% of total available study hours.

### Tier 3: Edge Cases & Quick-Skim (Bonus / Optional Marks)
- **Selection Criteria:** Niche subtopics, obscure derivations, or topics that appeared only once in the last 10 years.
- **Depth:** 10% Depth.
- **Coverage:** 1-page formula & keyword cheat sheet.
- **Time Allocated:** 10% of total available study hours (or skipped during Emergency Crunch).

## 3. Daily Study Time Allocation Matrix

When generating the daily schedule, divide daily hours into 3 distinct functional blocks:

1. **New Concept Intake & Intuition (50% of daily time):**
   - Active learning: Reading simplified breakdown, understanding the physical meaning, tracing step-by-step examples.
2. **Execution & Problem Solving (35% of daily time):**
   - Pen-on-paper practice: Solving past exam questions without looking at solutions, classifying items, drawing diagrams from memory.
3. **Active Recall & Retention Buffer (15% of daily time):**
   - Rapid-fire quiz, flashcard review, 5-minute self-explanation (Feynman test).

---

## 4. Emergency Crunch Triage Protocol (When $D \le 7$ Days)

When a student has only a few days before an exam:
1. **Rule 1: Stop reading textbooks from page 1.** Textbooks are linear; exams are non-linear.
2. **Rule 2: Anchor study directly to Past Exam Questions (PYQ reverse-engineering).**
3. **Rule 3: Master the "Part A / 2-Mark" definitions bank.** Part A questions are easy to score 100% on with minimal reading if core definitions are memorized.
4. **Rule 4: Standardize diagrams.** Pre-draw and memorize 3-5 versatile diagrams that can be inserted into multiple long-form answers to earn presentation marks.
